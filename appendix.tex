\section{Rule Mechanisms and Scope}
\label{sec:rules}

Rules construct candidates; exact-plan profiles determine admission in the current
default path. The historical experiments in this supplement used the earlier rule
planner. They do not validate the new selector. A retained instruction or a read-window
check constrains a transformation but does not prove semantic equivalence.

\textbf{ContextCompress.} The rule requires a prompt above 8,192 bytes and per-message
attention scores. Its IDF-based proxy identifies low-scoring messages for extractive
removal, subject to a minimum low-score fraction, a 50\% message-retention floor,
role-specific retention, protected user-input provenance, and system preservation.
The first user message is protected even if provenance is absent or incomplete.
The rule issues no auxiliary LLM compression request.

\textbf{StateDrop.} Explicit per-message state dependencies and an observed state-read
window identify state keys absent from reads since the previous LLM call. Such
messages are candidates for removal, subject to retention and provenance checks.
This is runtime pruning over supplied annotations, not sound whole-program liveness
analysis. It is unavailable to applications that supply neither state annotations
nor an equivalent adapter signal.

\textbf{Routing and output limits.} Configured compatible model targets enter the
complete candidate specification. Evidence for routing alone cannot admit the same
model after another rewrite. OutputBudget proposes a cap from profiled output-token
lengths when the application has no existing cap. A new cap changes plan identity
and needs evidence; short past outputs do not rule out future truncation.

\textbf{Other mechanisms.} PromptDedup targets repeated context. Structured and
dead-output truncation use additional structural or historical signals; no new
end-to-end efficacy result is claimed for them here. Cached responses require their
own policy and compatibility checks. Multi-call parallel plans are excluded from
the single-call profiled identity contract. Application-managed concurrency remains
outside the selector.

\section{Engineering Preflight and Reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

The archived native preflight, \path{bench/repro/joint-planner-preflight-2026-09-04.json},
uses four generated candidates and synthetic profiles with 20 paired observations.
Across three 20,000-decision runs it selects the expected jointly changed identity
60,000 times, with no fallback or unexpected identity. It measures a warm local
selector path; it excludes provider calls, cold learning, task grading, and realistic
storage contention. Its output is marked Stage~E0 and \texttt{paper\_evidence=false}.

The no-key campaign fixture runs ten arms over 160 synthetic cells with zero network
calls. It exercises arm isolation, canonical records, accounting namespaces, source
and task hashes, and resume consistency. Synthetic quality and cost values cannot
satisfy an empirical gate. The live campaign must independently pass interception,
request-preservation, grader, and full-accounting admission. Current exploratory
summaries are not the hierarchical, selection-adjusted confirmatory analysis.
