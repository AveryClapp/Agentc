Method
------
Release-built CPython 3.13 extension on the recorded arm64 macOS host. Five
replications x 2,000 timed steady-state calls per scenario (10,000 samples per
row), with 200 untimed warmup calls per replication and rotated scenario order.
The workload is deterministic and synthetic, exploration and shadow execution
are disabled, SQLite uses WAL, and no provider or network call occurs.

The outer clock encloses the complete `_native.optimize_plan` call. In the
current implementation, each call constructs and non-blockingly enqueues its
audit row; a single background writer batches up to 128 rows. An ordered flush
after each group places SQLite persistence outside the request-path clock and
allows exact span pairing. Python opens its separately linked SQLite only after
the native writer is closed. The internal clock still ends before audit-row
construction and excludes native state lookup plus the outer FFI boundary.

Audit accounting
----------------
Across setup and timed calls, 33,160 rows were attempted, accepted, and written.
After every ordered flush there were zero pending rows, full-queue drops,
disconnected drops, or write failures. The 30,000 timed rows pair exactly with
the raw samples.

Interpretation
--------------
Off-path persistence cuts the admitted and legacy p99 substantially and reduces
the paired residual to 12--15% of mean complete-call time. Reference p99 is
almost unchanged because its tail is now inside planner/scheduling work rather
than the removed SQLite commit. The residual combines the Python/Rust boundary,
native-state lookup, audit-row construction/enqueue, clock quantization, and
return conversion; it is not an audit-only timer.

The old 76 us / 120 us result aggregated 1,818 historical
`plan_audit.overhead_us` rows from different workloads and omitted both the old
audit write and outer FFI scope. It must be described as an internal
planner-clock diagnostic, not end-to-end optimizer overhead.

Evidence boundary
-----------------
Both artifacts have `paper_evidence=false`. They are zero-network,
single-machine Stage E0 systems diagnostics. They validate the timing mechanism,
request-path redesign, and measured-host loss accounting, but do not establish
task quality, savings, provider latency, concurrent scaling, or a confirmatory
campaign result.
