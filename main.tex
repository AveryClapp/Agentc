\begin{abstract}
An LLM agent runtime can reduce spending by changing a model, removing context,
or limiting output. These changes interact: compression alters the request a
router evaluates, and a cheaper model may respond differently to the compressed
request. Evidence for either change alone does not establish the behavior of their
combination. Agentc intercepts provider requests and selects complete alternatives
for individual calls while preserving the application's orchestration. Each
alternative has a versioned identity covering its model, ordered rewrites, cache,
output budget, and validation policy. The runtime admits an alternative using
observations for that exact plan, paired reference-response divergence, freshness,
and an exposure budget. Durable leases bound cold exploration; the original response
remains visible while evidence is acquired. We describe the implemented selector
and separate its engineering validation from historical rule experiments. Those
experiments show substantial input-token reductions on favorable fixtures, sparse
activation on natural prompts, and interference between rewrites. They motivate
complete-plan measurement but do not establish the selector's end-to-end benefit.
A frozen evaluation protocol specifies the independent workloads, controls, full
request accounting, and task-quality tests needed to establish that benefit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-step LLM agents issue a sequence of provider requests interleaved with tool
execution, state updates, and application decisions. A request may carry retrieved
passages, previous responses, and tool results, even when only part of that context
helps the next step. Its model and output limit may also be more expensive than the
step requires. Optimizing these requests can therefore reduce spending without
redesigning the application. The difficulty is deciding which changes to expose:
a plausible cheaper request can still damage a later decision or the final answer.

Routing and prompt rewriting are often evaluated separately. Their costs and
behavior are coupled. If compression reduces input length, it also reduces the
dollar value of routing to a lower input-token price. If it removes a cue used by
the smaller model, a route that worked on the original prompt can fail after the
rewrite. Conversely, eliminating distractors can improve a smaller model's answer.
Output caps create another interaction because routing can change response length.
Neither adding individual savings nor applying rules in a fixed order measures
these effects on the executed combination.

Related systems already optimize model allocations, prompts, and workflow
configurations~\cite{chen2025llmselector,he2025cognify,chaudhry2025murakkab}.
Client-side interception and routing also have direct prior
art~\cite{hua2026agentopt}. Agentc studies a narrower unit and deployment boundary:
\emph{a complete alternative for one intercepted provider call, admitted using
evidence for that alternative}. The application continues to choose its next step
and execute its tools. Agentc does not require control of the provider's weights,
KV cache, batching, or accelerator scheduler. This boundary permits integration
with hosted APIs, but also prevents the runtime from relying on those internal
signals to establish equivalence.

A complete plan includes the target model and the ordered request mutations that
will actually execute. Agentc gives that plan a stable, versioned identity and
keeps observations separate from evidence for other models, orders, or policies.
Candidates without adequate evidence receive bounded counterfactual execution:
the application receives the reference response, while a leased candidate request
collects paired observations. Once admitted, a candidate remains subject to sampled
reference comparison and a durable exposure guard. Inadequate evidence, incompatible
requests, expired profiles, and exhausted budgets lead to the original request.

This design addresses three concrete problems. First, it prevents a model-and-rewrite
combination from borrowing an apparently favorable score measured for only one
component. Second, it includes the expected cost of reference shadowing and dispatch
fallback in the admission screen. Third, it limits the cold experiments needed to
learn a plan instead of letting learning issue an unbounded number of requests.
These are properties of the implemented decision mechanism. Paired response
similarity remains a proxy; it cannot certify task success or bound harm on
unsampled calls.

The evidence in this draft has two distinct scopes. Local, no-provider checks
exercise exact-plan selection and isolated campaign orchestration. Earlier provider
experiments characterize individual rewrites and their activation regimes. For
example, ContextCompress reduces input tokens by 36.3\% on a purpose-built
long-context fixture, but activates on only one of 300 natural HotpotQA prompts.
An agent-level comparison also shows fewer compression activations when state
pruning is enabled. These results motivate measurement of complete plans rather
than an assumption of additive savings. They were collected for the earlier rule
planner and cannot establish the new selector's comparative value.

The paper develops the complete-plan identity and admission contract, its bounded
evidence-acquisition path, and the implemented request-boundary integration. It
then reports the available evidence with its limits and specifies the frozen
confirmatory evaluation. No held-out whole-system savings or task-quality
non-inferiority claim is made in the present draft.

\section{Optimization Boundary}
\label{sec:boundary}

\subsection{A Complete Plan for One Call}

Let $x$ be the request the application would send, and let $a_0(x)$ be that original
request, called the \emph{reference}. A candidate $a(x)$ is one concrete provider
request or a compatible cached response. For a provider request, the alternative
can change the model, permitted context messages, or the output-token cap. A plan
is complete when these choices and the dispatch and validation policies needed to
execute them are specified. Complete does not mean optimal across an agent's future
trajectory: later calls depend on the response the application receives, and Agentc
does not search alternative tool or control-flow trajectories.

The economic objective is to reduce all billed work while satisfying a task-quality
constraint. At deployment time, however, task labels and a workflow evaluator may
be unavailable. Agentc can observe request usage, elapsed time, dispatch fallbacks,
and divergence between responses to the same call. Its admission procedure therefore
uses an empirical response-divergence threshold, then requires separate benchmark
validation of end-to-end task quality. The threshold is an operational screen,
not a substitution for the task-quality constraint.

A simple input-only example illustrates why the evidence unit matters. With $I$
input tokens, input price $p_0$, compressed fraction $r$, and alternative price $p_1$,
the combined input charge is $(1-r)Ip_1$. Adding compression savings $rIp_0$ to
routing savings $I(p_0-p_1)$ overstates the combined savings by $rI(p_0-p_1)$.
This algebraic interaction occurs before considering output length, caches,
shadow calls, or quality. Empirical complete-plan observations also capture those
response-dependent effects for observed executions; they do not predict unseen
combinations without evidence.

\subsection{Compatibility and Scope}

The interceptor preserves the provider protocol and application-managed orchestration.
Structured output schemas, tool definitions, tool-call identifiers, native content
blocks, and streaming requirements constrain which candidates are legal. Opaque
content is eligible only for transformations that preserve it. An unsupported
protocol or request shape remains a reference call. The profiled selector excludes
multi-call parallel plans because its identity and evidence contract describe one
request alternative; it does not treat one branch as evidence for a whole parallel
execution.

Provenance supplies additional restrictions. A message can originate from user
input, a tool, an earlier LLM response, an explicit state key, or a literal. Rules
must preserve protected input and system instructions. ContextCompress also retains
the first user message when provenance metadata is absent or incomplete, avoiding
reliance on an adapter having annotated every instruction. StateDrop needs explicit
state-read and state-write annotations; interception alone cannot establish which
state the application consumes. These checks constrain candidate construction.
They do not prove that removing an unprotected passage preserves meaning.

The default optimization path is the profiled complete-plan selector. The earlier
greedy planner remains an explicit comparison mode. Its projected savings and
rule-local observations are not silently promoted into complete-plan evidence.

\subsection{Versioned Plan and Call-Site Identities}
\label{sec:identity}

Evidence is indexed by a pair $(v,k)$: a call-site version $v$ and an execution-plan
identity $k$. The call-site version includes the stable call site, prompt shape,
provider protocol, tool-schema version, and application-configuration version.
Prompt shape represents roles, dependency kinds, and request structure rather than
the raw prompt text. Changes to tools or generation configuration create a new
evidence cohort instead of reusing an old site's measurements.

The plan identity is a hash of a canonical, schema-versioned specification. It
covers the requested and target models, an available target revision, ordered
rewrites with implementation versions and parameters, cache policy, output budget,
and validation policy. Rewrite order is significant in the live path. The generic
identity format permits canonical sorting only when a generator explicitly certifies
commutation; it does not infer commutation from a shared cost-driver category.
The divergence mode, threshold, and sampling policy are also identity-bearing
because they affect which plan is admitted and how much reference work it incurs.

Prices are versioned observations rather than semantic identity fields. Runtime
metadata records the provider protocol, observed target-model version, and price-table
version. A mismatch makes a profile cold. For a hosted alias without an immutable
revision, the catalog records a versioned alias cohort; this does not pin a hidden
provider implementation. Content-free identity fields also do not make the trace
store content-free: request and response storage needs its own deployment policy.

\subsection{Bounded Candidate Construction}
\label{sec:candidates}

The reference is always the first candidate. A disabled optimizer or a call site
below the hot threshold produces only that reference. Otherwise, rules inspect the
request and its profile, propose concrete mutations, and enforce compatibility,
provenance, and retention checks. The generator enumerates compatible sets of up
to three semantic rewrites, preserving their order, and crosses those alternatives
with compatible configured model targets. This produces reference, route-only,
rewrite-only, and joint model-and-rewrite candidates.

Examples span distinct mechanisms. ContextCompress removes low-scoring messages;
StateDrop removes explicitly tagged state whose key is absent from the observed
read window; PromptDedup addresses redundant messages; OutputBudget sets a cap
from observed output lengths. These rules are proposals, not proof of equivalent
behavior. A structural precondition can miss an opportunity, and a retained message
can still lose useful surrounding context. The supplementary material gives rule
mechanics and historical isolation results.

Rule-local savings estimates help prune proposals, but do not rank the resulting
complete plans. In particular, ModelDowngrade is represented by the target model
in the complete plan rather than applied again as an independent semantic rewrite.
Enumeration is bounded by a wall-clock deadline, with a 5\,ms production planning
budget. If candidate construction or selection exceeds that budget, the runtime
uses the reference. The search therefore makes no claim of finding the globally
best rewrite set, model, or full agent execution.

\subsection{Exact-Plan Profiles}
\label{sec:profiles}

Each $(v,k)$ profile retains a rolling window of execution outcomes and paired
reference comparisons. The default window holds the newest 50 samples of each
kind. Outcome records contain input and output tokens, elapsed time, measured or
price-derived cost, and dispatch-fallback information. Paired records contain
response divergence and their timestamps. Lifetime counts are reported separately;
old samples outside the retained window do not satisfy the evidence requirement.
Profiles are persisted and hydrated with their runtime versions.

An opaque observation token ties delayed feedback to the exact site, plan, runtime
versions, and execution sequence. This avoids crediting an asynchronous completion
to whichever plan happens to be selected later. Duplicate identical feedback is
idempotent; conflicting feedback is rejected. The unit of storage matters when
multiple candidates coexist at one site: routing evidence for the original prompt
cannot admit the same target model after a context rewrite.

For retained paired divergences $d_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq d_{(n)}$, the implemented
screen uses the order statistic
\begin{equation}
 U_a=d_{(\min(n,\lceil0.95(n+1)\rceil))}.
\end{equation}
At the default minimum of 20 paired observations, this is the maximum retained
divergence. It is a conservative empirical screen at that sample size, not a
formal task-quality confidence bound. Its interpretation depends on the divergence
metric and on whether recent sampled calls represent future traffic. Prompt content
can drift within the same structural cohort.

\subsection{Admission and Ranking}
\label{sec:admission}

A non-reference candidate needs a compatible, enabled plan; a current reference
profile; at least 20 retained paired comparisons; and a latest paired observation
within 24 hours. Its estimates must be finite and valid, its divergence screen must meet
the plan-specific threshold, and its accumulated exposure must remain below the
configured budget. Invalid configuration or a missing profile causes abstention.
Optional absolute cost and latency limits add deployment constraints.

Let $\bar C_0$ and $\bar C_a$ be reference and candidate mean cost, $s$ the configured
reference-shadow rate, and $f_a$ the observed dispatch-fallback fraction. The
production cost screen requires positive estimated benefit:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat B_a=\bar C_0-\bar C_a-(s+f_a)\bar C_0>0.
 \label{eq:benefit}
\end{equation}
The analogous latency screen also subtracts planner overhead. This is an admission
approximation based on observed profiles, not an all-calls billing ledger. Cold
exploration, evaluator calls, and retries not present in those profiles still need
to be charged in a whole-system measurement. The default live objective is cost;
the generic selector also supports latency objectives.

Among admitted plans, the selector ranks by expected candidate mean cost for the
cost objective. Ties prefer more paired evidence, lower divergence, fewer mutations,
and then the stable plan identity. It does not sum per-rule savings or maximize a
claimed workflow-wide benefit. Equation~\ref{eq:benefit} is an eligibility check;
ranking by candidate mean cost is distinct from ranking by a complete billed-cost
ledger. If no candidate is admitted, the original request executes.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Request-boundary selection and evidence acquisition}
\label{alg:selection}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item Preserve the reference request and derive its call-site version.
\item Enumerate compatible complete candidates within the planning deadline.
\item Load exact-plan profiles; reject stale, unsupported, disabled, under-evidenced,
or non-beneficial alternatives.
\item Select the lowest-cost admitted candidate, or the reference if none qualifies.
\item If reference is selected, reserve a bounded cold-evidence lease when eligible.
The application still receives the reference response.
\item Recheck the exposure guard before exposing a selected alternative; dispatch
and correlate its outcome with the exact observation token.
\item Record leased or sampled paired feedback; update profiles and exposure state.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Cold Evidence Without Candidate Exposure}
\label{sec:exploration}

A selector that requires exact-plan evidence cannot admit an unseen alternative
merely because an individual rule scored well. Agentc acquires that evidence with
a separate cold-exploration controller. At a call site, compatible under-evidenced
candidates can receive counterfactual provider requests while the original response
continues through the application. Candidate tool calls are not used to advance
the application's trajectory in this reference-visible mode.

The default controller permits 20 cold attempts per site in a rolling 24-hour
window and one concurrent lease per site. It aims for 20 paired observations per
candidate. Reservation uses a SQLite transaction and occurs before dispatch;
failed attempts consume the budget, and abandoned leases expire after ten minutes.
The cap survives process restarts and competing workers. Candidate ordering favors
less paired evidence and fewer attempts, followed by deterministic seeded priority
and a stable identity. Disabled or incompatible plans are ineligible.

These defaults expose an explicit learning tradeoff. A site's daily allowance can
fully profile one cold candidate, not every model-and-rewrite combination. Splitting
attempts across alternatives can leave all of them below the admission threshold.
The controller bounds attempted work; it does not guarantee adequate evidence or
reserve an exact dollar amount before a response arrives. Costly candidates and
long responses can still make a bounded experiment expensive. Exploration spending
must be amortized and reported rather than removed as warmup.

\subsection{Exposure Guard and Sampled Validation}
\label{sec:guard}

After admission, sampled reference execution updates paired divergence and a
durable guard for the exact $(v,k)$ plan. With threshold $\tau_a$, the guard tracks
positive excess divergence over its rolling 24-hour window:
\begin{equation}
 E_a=\sum_i\max(0,d_i-\tau_a).
 \label{eq:exposure}
\end{equation}
The default exposure budget is 1.0; reaching it disables the plan. A final guard
check before exposure handles a concurrent disable decision. Identical repeated
feedback does not increase the sum twice. Invalid or conflicting feedback does
not silently change the evidence.

The default admitted-plan reference-shadow rate is 2\%. This rate is separate from
the cold controller's lease budget. At sparse sampling, harmful calls can occur
before a reference comparison detects them; a low divergence metric can also miss
a task-critical error. Equation~\ref{eq:exposure} bounds the recorded excess signal,
not unsampled task damage. Detection delay and the relationship between divergence
and labels require explicit evaluation.

A cooldown alone does not re-enable a disabled plan. The guard's readmission API
is intended for use after fresh cold admission and cooldown, but automatic
readmission is not wired into the
current serving path. A disabled plan therefore remains blocked until explicit
readmission. This conservative recovery behavior should be included in activation
and long-run cost measurements.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

Agentc implements candidate construction, identities, profiles, selection, and
exposure state in Rust. PyO3 bindings expose these mechanisms to a Python interceptor
that patches supported provider SDK paths and framework adapters. The dispatcher
executes the selected request or returns the original call path. The application
retains responsibility for tool execution, retries it initiates, state updates,
and concurrent callbacks. State-sensitive rules need the additional annotations
described in Section~\ref{sec:boundary}.

The interception boundary creates practical failure cases. A framework can bypass
an SDK patch through a direct HTTP client. A provider can return opaque content that
a rewrite cannot reconstruct. Streaming responses need usage and completion feedback
without losing native events. The current evaluation harness includes SDK, LiteLLM,
and HTTP admission probes to determine which calls were actually intercepted.
Framework support is therefore established per worker and protocol path, not inferred
from importing Agentc successfully.

Evaluation configuration must also isolate learning state. Each campaign arm uses
its own subprocess and optimizer storage namespace. Complete-plan observation,
reference shadowing, cold exploration, and greedy control modes are explicit.
Source and task hashes, arm definitions, repetition IDs, and selection locks are
recorded so a resumed campaign cannot silently mix experiments. These controls
make the comparison inspectable; they do not supply its missing provider outcomes.

\section{Available Evidence}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{What Has Been Validated}

The current engineering path has a no-key campaign fixture and a native selector
preflight. The fixture completed 160 cells across ten arms with isolated processes,
canonical records, and zero network calls. It tests campaign execution and integrity;
its synthetic outcomes carry no efficacy evidence. The native preflight constructs
four complete candidates, persists a reference and one jointly changed profile,
and exercises warm exact-plan selection. Its archived three runs select the expected
plan on all 60,000 measured decisions. Synthetic costs and divergences make this a
check of identity, hydration, and selector behavior, not a measurement of provider
savings, task quality, cold acquisition, or full-request latency.

The historical experiments below predate the current default selector. They use
shared task baselines and configuration-specific optimizer state, with warmup before
measurement. Several use purpose-built structure that makes a rule activate. Their
scope is rule characterization, and their warmup-excluded savings cannot be treated
as intention-to-treat whole-system savings. The appendix preserves the isolation
matrices and data-source mapping; Table~\ref{tab:evidence} summarizes the principal
observations needed for the present argument.

\subsection{Activation and Attribution}

On the purpose-built long-context fixture, disabling ContextCompress eliminates the
observed input-token reduction; enabling only that rule reduces input tokens by
36.3\%. The all-on reduction is 34.0\%. This isolation connects the reduction to
message removal within the fixture. It does not establish how often natural agent
traffic offers comparable removable context. On unexpanded HotpotQA prompts, whose
median size is 6,940 bytes, CC activates only once in 300 measured tasks and reduces
aggregate input tokens by 0.19\%. A compression opportunity can therefore exist
without meeting the runtime's activation conditions.

StateDrop's historical state fixture provides a separate precondition check. When
all state keys are included in the observed read window, it fires zero times in
320 measured calls. Under the standard fixture it fires 116 times in 320 calls.
This demonstrates dependence on supplied read-window metadata, not sound static
liveness analysis. Input-token changes are useful for attribution because stochastic
output lengths can dominate small cost differences. ModelDowngrade, by contrast,
changes token prices while leaving input tokens approximately unchanged; its
historical routing fixture reports 11.23\,mUSD less recorded cost and a $-$3.9\,pp accuracy
change. The nonsignificant accuracy test does not establish preservation.

\subsection{Interactions in Historical Agent Runs}

The Autogen bridge experiment uses a two-call agent and 300 tasks with a shared
baseline of 229 correct outcomes. CC-only produces 225 correct outcomes; all-on
produces 226. The combined per-task sidecar contains both configurations with
matching task IDs and baseline outcomes. Exact paired McNemar tests give
$p=0.2891$ for CC-only (six tasks broken, two fixed) and $p=0.5488$ for all-on
(seven broken, four fixed). Task-paired percentile bootstrap intervals with 5,000
resamples are $[-3.33,+0.33]$\,pp and $[-3.33,+1.00]$\,pp, respectively. These
unadjusted descriptive intervals allow meaningful regression; nonsignificance is
not evidence of non-inferiority.

The summary artifact records input-token sums of 2,211,824 for baseline,
1,636,673 for CC-only, and 1,901,898 for all-on. These imply reductions of 26.0\%
and 14.0\% in captured spans. The harness permits incomplete span export and the
original span-count denominators are unavailable. The historical 23.5\% per-call
claim is therefore withdrawn; the available sums cannot certify complete-workload
spending or per-call savings.

Reported CC activations decrease from 350 to 184 of 600 nominal calls when StateDrop
is also enabled; reported StateDrop activations are 260. This is consistent with
state pruning changing whether the remaining request exceeds the compression
threshold. It motivates measuring the concrete executed combination. The artifacts
do not isolate this mechanism causally, and the experiment is not a comparison of
joint selection against routing-only, static-joint, or sequential controls.

Small composition fixtures have further limits. Two $n=20$ model-and-compression
runs report different routing contributions, while an $n=30$ compression-and-state
experiment has only a 0.06\% standalone input-token contribution from StateDrop.
Neither establishes a reproducible material end-to-end composition gain. They remain
supplementary observations rather than headline evidence for the new selector.

\section{Frozen Confirmatory Evaluation}
\label{sec:methodology}

The main empirical question remains open: does exact complete-plan selection lower
all-calls spending, at acceptable task quality, relative to strong feasible controls
on independent agent workloads? A frozen protocol separates engineering admission,
calibration, sealed pilot, and confirmatory stages. The specification predates this
rewrite. No confirmatory outcome is reported in this draft.

\subsection{Workloads, Arms, and Isolation}

The protocol targets independent tool-use, software-repair, and desktop-action
workloads, represented by $\tau$-Knowledge on $\tau^2$-Bench~\cite{shi2026tauknowledge,barres2026tau2},
SWE-bench-family agents~\cite{jimenez2024swebench,badertdinov2025swerebench}, and
OSWorld 2.0~\cite{yuan2026osworld2}. Upstream prompts, tools, and application control flow remain unchanged;
installation happens through launcher and interception paths. A workload enters
measurement only after an engineering probe establishes interception coverage,
request preservation, grading validity, and accounting for all relevant actors.
Tau and SWE sources are pinned for engineering checks. Live worker and grader
admission remains unfinished, and desktop-workload admission is also pending.

Ten arms distinguish the proposed mechanism from simpler policies:
unmodified strong model; trace-only strong model; fixed cheap model; routing-only;
rewrite-only on the strong model; calibration-selected static joint configuration;
route-then-rewrite; rewrite-then-route; the current greedy planner; and the profiled
joint selector with 2\% reference shadowing. Calibration alone chooses static
configurations and the strongest admissible comparison. Selection is locked before
held-out evaluation; the greedy and joint modes use separate state. All arms receive
matched tasks and repetition IDs. Primary comparisons require at least five
repetitions per task, and an incomplete arm-task-repetition cell is not silently
removed from the comparison.

\subsection{Accounting and Task Quality}

Efficiency counts every billed request: visible execution, reference shadows,
cold counterfactuals, retries, fallback work, and any separate user-simulator or
judge requests. Actor labels keep judges and simulators out of the optimizer while
retaining their cost in the ledger. The primary endpoint is dollars for tool-use
and software-repair workloads, and wall-clock latency for the desktop workload
with dollars secondary. Accounting must retain provider-reported cached and uncached
input, output, and reasoning usage with the corresponding versioned prices and
cache configuration. Cold-start and zero-activation tasks remain in the assigned
population. The current selector's cost approximation in
Equation~\ref{eq:benefit} is not the confirmatory accounting rule.

Task quality uses the upstream grader. Prespecified non-inferiority margins are
three percentage points for tool-use reward and two points for software resolution
and normalized desktop score. The lower endpoint of a two-sided 95\% paired
interval must exceed the corresponding negative margin. A nonsignificant superiority
test cannot pass this gate. The protocol requires at least 80\% estimated power
and reports failed, censored, and budget-exhausted executions in the assigned
population.

Analysis resamples tasks and then their matched repetitions in a hierarchical paired
bootstrap with 10,000 resamples. It reports geometric mean cost or latency ratios
alongside arithmetic aggregate cost differences, without removing retry tails.
Calibration selection is repeated inside the bootstrap for comparisons against the
chosen admissible control. Holm adjustment covers the five primary cells; secondary
analyses use the prespecified false-discovery procedure. The campaign runner's
current exploratory summaries do not implement these confirmatory conclusions.

\subsection{Guard Utility and Learning Cost}

The sparse guard needs its own labeled evaluation. The protocol sweeps admitted-plan
shadow rates of 0, 1, 2, 5, 10, and 100\%, across harmful and benign shifts in each
plan family. Replay sequences and a live subset measure detection delay, excess
labeled task damage, benign disable rate, and reference spending. The proposed 2\%
operating point must meet the frozen harm-detection and cost-retention gates;
dense-shadow success cannot be substituted for those results.

Complete-plan profiling also needs an amortization study. Report calls and dollars
to first admission, the share of sites that never acquire adequate evidence, retained
window turnover, and savings after charging cold attempts. Compare these quantities
across candidate counts and drift rates. Exact identities can prevent evidence
contamination while fragmenting the available samples. A selector that abstains
correctly on every task can pass some engineering checks while offering no useful
economic gain; activation and total cost therefore belong beside quality.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Client-side and workflow optimization.}
AgentOpt documents HTTP interception, tracking, caching, and evaluated model-combination
search without changing agent code~\cite{hua2026agentopt}. Its current repository
also documents per-call routing. Cognify jointly tunes structure, models, code, and
prompts using a supplied evaluator and search budget; that evaluator can be an LLM
judge~\cite{he2025cognify}. LLMSelector studies model allocations for compound
systems and uses question--desired-answer pairs, with an LLM diagnoser replacing
missing module-level labels~\cite{chen2025llmselector}. These systems occupy broad
claims about transparent optimization, joint search, and model complementarity.
Agentc's comparison concerns runtime admission from exact call-plan observations
and paired reference divergence when no workflow evaluator is supplied at deployment.
Comparative effectiveness remains to be measured.

\textbf{Runtime and serving systems.}
Agentix reconstructs dynamically revealed program execution and controls serving-engine
scheduling and routing while leaving application logic
unchanged~\cite{luo2026agentix}. Halo plans placement and scheduling for structured
DAGs, including batch and online streaming execution~\cite{shen2026halo}. Murakkab
jointly optimizes workflow, model/tool, hardware, and routing choices using profiles
and adaptive execution, including hosted proprietary
models~\cite{chaudhry2025murakkab}. Its dynamic request support means that the
absence of a user-declared static DAG is not sufficient to distinguish Agentc.
The supported boundary is preservation of the existing application's orchestration
while selecting request alternatives. Serving optimizations such as PagedAttention
and SGLang work below that boundary~\cite{kwon2023vllm,zheng2024sglang} and may
complement request-level changes.

\textbf{Approximation, routing, compression, and caching.}
ApproxMLIR unifies ML and non-ML approximation knobs and includes state-dependent
runtime decisions and a try-check-recover contract~\cite{ren2026approxmlir}. Runtime
guards and joint quality/performance optimization are therefore established ideas;
Agentc applies an empirical evidence contract to intercepted API alternatives.
RouteLLM and FrugalGPT optimize model use through routing or
cascades~\cite{ong2025routellm,chen2024frugalgpt}. LLMLingua methods and RECOMP reduce
prompt context~\cite{jiang2023llmlingua,pan2024llmlingua2,xu2024recomp}; semantic caches
reuse related answers~\cite{bang2023gptcache,gill2025meancache,schroeder2026vcache}.
These mechanisms provide candidate actions or relevant controls. Combining them
requires evidence for the executed alternative and fair accounting of their own
training, search, inference, and validation work.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The principal limitation is empirical: the current complete-plan selector has not
passed the frozen held-out comparisons. Historical rule experiments and synthetic
engineering checks do not answer that question. The most favorable compression
fixture deliberately exposes removable messages, whereas natural HotpotQA largely
abstains. Autogen span completeness is unresolved. These boundaries prevent a
claim of broad savings or task-quality preservation.

The runtime uses a bounded candidate set and recent empirical profiles. It can miss
better alternatives, reject low-frequency sites, or fragment evidence across versions
and rewrite orders. Freshness checks the latest paired timestamp; the retained
count window can still contain older samples. A prompt-shape cohort does not capture
every semantic change; sparse response comparisons can miss rare task-critical errors. Reference output
can itself be wrong, and similarity to it need not imply quality. Divergence and
exposure thresholds are deployment parameters requiring calibration and labeled
stress tests, not semantic correctness guarantees.

Cold exploration is reference-visible but still incurs provider work. Its attempt
cap is not a hard dollar reservation, and the production admission screen does not
amortize all acquisition or external evaluator costs. The live path ranks candidate
mean cost after a benefit screen; future ledger-aware ranking should be evaluated
before claiming minimum total spending. Automatic guarded-plan readmission remains
unwired. Provider aliases can drift without an exposed revision, and unsupported
SDK or HTTP paths can bypass interception. State pruning requires annotations.
Historical fixtures do not isolate provider-native prefix-cache effects from
changes in prompt content.
These issues must appear in coverage, abstention, recovery, and cost results rather
than being hidden by warmup or successful-task filtering.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Agentc implements evidence-guided selection of complete model-and-rewrite alternatives
at the provider-request boundary. Exact identities, bounded cold acquisition, and
per-plan admission prevent isolated rule scores from standing in for evidence on
the executed combination. Historical experiments motivate this unit of measurement
and reveal narrow activation regimes and interactions. Establishing a useful
whole-system result still requires the frozen independent-workload comparisons,
full request accounting, task-quality non-inferiority tests, and sparse-guard stress
evaluation.
